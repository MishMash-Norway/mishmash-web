The text comes from the captions of the recording, joined into paragraphs, with the names of the programme put back where the captions misheard them. The music is not described; the [lab page](/lab/opening-ceremony/) plays the recording from the start of any part.

## 18:10 Dancing Embryo

*Diego Marin, University of Oslo; Benedikte Wallace, University of Oslo*

<div lang="en" markdown="1">
The following performance presents neither a video projected for the dancer nor a choreography composed in advance. The avatar does not move under direct human control. It is a digital model trained with motion capture data. Its movement is not a mere reproduction of recorded material, but the result of algorithmic transformations that reorganize it into synthetic configurations, glitches of body movement that exceed human biomechanical limits. The qualities of the avatar's movement are modulated in real time by the gestures of the human dancer, allowing both to engage in a dynamic loop of embodied interaction. To do this, the dancer follows a specific methodology, not only shaping the movements of the AI, but also allowing himself to be shaped in return by the behavior of the avatar. What emerges is a human-AI kinematic relationship that exists only through the act of dancing.

This work embraces the fragility of technology, revealing the tense and unpredictable coupling between human and artificial rhythms of movement. It invites us to question what it means to dance with another, to reconsider the concept of the body when its presence is digital, and to examine how collaborative creation can emerge between a sentient dancer and one that is not.
