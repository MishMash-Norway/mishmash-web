MishMash works in the open by default. This page sets out the centre's commitments for publications, data and code.

## Why "open research" and not "open science"

The centre uses the term *open research* rather than *open science* deliberately. Artistic research produces exhibitions, performances, scores, instruments and documentation, not only articles and datasets. A term that covers only science would leave out much of what MishMash makes, and the people who make it.

## Publications

MishMash results are generally published in open access channels, often with early versions deposited in preprint archives so that the work is readable before a journal's process is finished. Given the breadth of the centre, outputs range from monographs and book chapters to journal articles and conference papers.

We encourage co-authorship across disciplines and institutions. The level of co-publishing can be seen as a proxy for how well we engage in interdisciplinary and interinstitutional dialogue.

## Data

Research data should follow the FAIR principles, which means that they should be *findable*, *accessible*, *interoperable*, and *reusable*. Datasets are deposited in trusted repositories with a persistent identifier, such as [DataverseNO](https://dataverse.no/) or [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/communities/mishmash/), and registered in [NVA](https://nva.sikt.no/), which is where the [results page](/results/) is generated from. Artistic material belongs in repositories for creative practice, such as [Freesound](https://freesound.org/) and the [Research Catalogue](https://www.researchcatalogue.net/). Material from libraries and museums connects to the European cultural heritage infrastructure.

Data are released in standard, open file formats, with the documentation and software needed to read them, and with a data management plan. The rule is that data should be shared "as open as possible and as closed as necessary", following the dataset's privacy and copyright limitations. MishMash discusses with relevant partners how this can be done in practice.

Where the centre can choose, data carry a permissive licence, normally [CC BY](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The site's own data are published at [mishmash.no/data/](/data/).

## Code

Software and hardware developed in MishMash are released as open source, on public platforms, and under permissive licences. Licensing is under discussion in the network, since the interests of a freelancer, an institution and a company are not the same.

The website follows the same approach: its code is MIT-licensed and available on [GitHub](https://github.com/MishMash-Norway/mishmash-web), while the content (text, images, etc.) is CC BY, as set out in the [terms of use](/about/terms/).

## Why it matters

Open research is a requirement from our main funder (Research Council of Norway). However, MishMash sees it as more than a requirement. Permissive licences make tools available to people and institutions that cannot buy them, in Norway and abroad. This is part of what the centre means by working responsibly with AI.
