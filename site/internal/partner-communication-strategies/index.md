## AHO

No communication strategy is public. The strategy page lists Strategy 2026-2036, planning documents and rules, and among the rules the language policy guidelines, published 24 March 2026. The language policy covers academic work, administrative communication and "digital dissemination", explicitly including social media. It requires all public communication to follow the Language Council's plain language principles (clear, correct and adapted to the user), sets Norwegian as the main language in both written forms, and allows English only when academically motivated. The AI guidelines (published 28 March 2025, updated 30 June 2026) apply to students and staff, describe AI as a support tool rather than an answer key, and require citing the tool and the prompt when text is generated, and disclosing AI use in translation and in image captions. Nothing on open science or research communication. Searched: kommunikasjonsstrategi, the strategy page, språkpolitiske retningslinjer.

## BI

No communication strategy, language policy or social media guideline is public. Strategy 2030 has one relevant goal: "Enhance visibility and recognition of BI's research through intensified engagement in the public debate, media outlets, research communication and practice-oriented channels." No mention of plain language, open science, social media or AI in communication. Searched: kommunikasjonsstrategi, retningslinjer sosiale medier, strategy 2030.

## HiØ

## HVL

No communication strategy is public. The language policy guidelines (revised October 2025) make Nynorsk the main language, require at least 25% of each written form, give Norwegian Sign Language a priority status, treat English as a parallel language, and state that plain language applies to all written communication. No mention of social media, AI or research communication beyond publication language. Searched: kommunikasjonsstrategi, språkprofil and sosiale medier, kommunikasjonsplan and språkpolitikk.

## IFE

No separate communication strategy. The Corporate Strategy 2025-2029 (external version) has a section titled Role in society and communication. Ambition 7 of ten is "Actively participate in public discourse within our areas of expertise", with three goals: take part in boards and committees where framework conditions are set, strengthen reputation and awareness of the social mission, and "Be visible in the media and on other public platforms within our areas of expertise". Stakeholders listed range from international bodies and authorities to the media, neighbours and tenants. On the nuclear clean-up the strategy says that "Through proactive communication and public engagement, IFE will ensure that the public and authorities are kept informed". AI is discussed as a research field and as an administrative tool used "to a limited extent", not as a communication issue. Nothing on plain language or open science. Searched: kommunikasjonsstrategi, strategi and formidling, corporate strategy.

## KHiO

The only communication strategy on the site is a 2013 draft for a leadership meeting (Kommunikasjonsstrategi for Kunsthøgskolen i Oslo mot 2016). Purpose: to strengthen the academy's overall reputation and position locally, nationally and internationally, and to strengthen staff and student pride. Audiences: students, staff, the owner (the ministry) and society. Five goals: better internal communication; effective and targeted external communication; first choice for the best applicants; greater visibility for artistic research; a stronger position as a societal actor. It adopts the state communication policy (openness, reaching all affected, coordinated, active, participatory) and notes the lack of a shared platform for making artistic research accessible. Social media appear as marketing channels, and it asks for bilingual documentation. A separate language policy (adopted 30 September 2014) sets parallel language use and requires both written forms to be visible, without a plain language clause. Strategy 2023-2028 states that the academy has "an important voice in the public sphere". Searched: kommunikasjonsstrategi (three queries), språkpolitikk, strategi 2023-2028.

## Kristiania

No communication strategy is public. The language policy guidelines (adopted 17 December 2020, updated 4 April 2025) make plain language the basis for written communication, require high language quality in all communication and dissemination, expect research to be published in English while much dissemination targets a Norwegian audience, and promise training in research communication. ## Nord University

Communication Strategy for Nord University 2024-2030, passed by the board on 5 December 2024, in Bokmål, Nynorsk and English. Purpose: to use communication as a strategic tool for Strategy 2030 and make strong research environments and the university visible. Audiences: prospective students (young, international, continuing education), staff, partners, national media and public debate. Principles: "All our students and all employees at Nord University are communication workers"; the state communication policy and the line principle apply; researchers are responsible for communicating their own research; four areas (student recruitment, research dissemination, corporate communication, internal communication) are followed by annual action plans with target groups and measurement of effect. Social media are to be used "strategically to recruit students"; popular science dissemination is promoted; Sami language responsibility is to be made visible. No mention of plain language, open science or AI. Searched: kommunikasjonsstrategi, the communication unit page, the strategy PDF.

## Common themes

1. The state communication policy is the shared foundation. KHiO, NMH, Nord, OsloMet, UiA and UiB all cite Statens kommunikasjonspolitikk, and its five principles (openness, reaching all affected, coherence, being active, participation) recur almost verbatim.
2. Communication is everyone's job under the line principle. NTNU, Nord, OsloMet, UiB and UiT all place responsibility with leaders and staff, and Nord and UiT say explicitly that researchers must disseminate their own research.
3. Reputation and national visibility are the stated reason for the strategy. UiA, Nord, UiT and KHiO frame the document around position and reputation; NILU, IFE, BI and Kristiania make visibility an ambition in their general strategies.
4. Openness is meant as meroffentlighet and public debate, not as open science. NTNU, UiB, UiO, IFE, Simula and SINTEF commit to taking part in public discourse; only UiO (open access) and Simula (open source and open data) connect communication to open research practice.
5. Plain language lives in language policies rather than in communication strategies. AHO, NMH, HVL, Kristiania and UiA require klarspråk through language guidelines or the Language Act; UiB is the only communication document that says it in its own words.
6. Social media are treated as a recruitment channel with operational rules. Nord and UiA use them for student recruitment; NTNU and UiB give unit-level rules (two administrators, answer the same day, plan each post) rather than a stance.
7. Many core documents are old. NTNU (2012), KHiO (2013), UiO (2015), OsloMet (2016) and UiB (2017) predate their current strategies; only Nord, UiT and UiA have documents from 2024 or later.
8. Measurement is promised but not shown. UiO, OsloMet and Nord say effects should be measured, and none of the fetched documents publishes an indicator or a result.

## What none of them do

1. Rules for AI in the institution's own communication. UiA has one sentence on responsible AI use and AHO regulates student work, but no document says whether AI-generated text, images or translations in official communication are disclosed. A centre on AI and creativity can state a disclosure rule and follow it.
2. Open science, open education and open knowledge as communication principles. None of the documents licenses its communication material for reuse, links every story to its data or code, treats teaching material as something to publish openly, or gives facts back to Wikidata and the commons; UiO's one line on open access and Simula's on open source and open data are the nearest. MishMash can make sources, licences and machine-readable terms part of every piece, publish its teaching material openly, contribute to the open knowledge graph, and open the door to citizen contributions with clear terms.
3. Communicating limits and uncertainty. Only UiO says the university must communicate the limits of research. A centre working on AI can commit to showing what a system cannot do, and how a result was measured, as a standing rule.
4. Art as subject, object, expression and method. The arts institutions (KHiO, NMH) treat concerts and exhibitions as activities to be advertised, and other institutions sometimes use them to disseminate science done elsewhere. In MishMash art is what the centre studies, what it makes, how it expresses what it finds and how it inquires, so artistic work is communication in its own right and is judged as research as well as outreach.
5. Published measurements. No partner publishes the numbers behind its communication goals. MishMash can publish a small set of generated, checkable indicators and let the strategy be judged against them.
