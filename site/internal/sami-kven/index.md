Two documents that would have been useful could not be read: the Sami Parliament's guidelines on artificial intelligence (the page body is loaded by script and was empty when fetched) and a 2016 Oslo Economics report on automatic translation in the state sector (it turned out to concern the EU's MT@EC service and contains no mention of Sami or Kven).

## Summary

1. The Language Act (2021) makes Sami and Norwegian equal in value (§ 5) and names Kven a national minority language (§ 6); the operative duty for a state body is in § 1, to protect and promote both, and Språkrådet reads that duty as applying to every public body, including those outside the Sami administrative area, with a website in a Sami language given as an example of promotion.
2. The Sami Act chapter 3 gives individual rights only inside the administrative area (14 municipalities in three tiers) and covers North, Lule and South Sami; the fetched text exempts national bodies from the right to a Sami reply unless a regulation says otherwise, so a university in Oslo has no translation obligation under it, only the general duty above.
3. Kven carries no individual right to services or information; the bill behind the Act says so in as many words, and the duty is to support the language through measures. UiT holds the national responsibility for North Sami and Kven and says so in its language policy; Nord holds Lule and South Sami.
4. Machine translation into a Sami language is offered by exactly one public system, the University of Tartu's Neurotõlge (smugri3, CC-BY-4.0, 27 languages including Norwegian and five Sami languages, a hosted API and a Docker worker), whose authors warn that quality can be especially low into low-resource languages.
5. Giellatekno's Apertium system translates only from North Sami to Norwegian and refuses the other direction on purpose, stating that the quality would be far from publishable and would harm the language. That package is in Ubuntu 24.04, so it would run in GitHub Actions, but in the wrong direction for a mirror.
6. Kven has a dictionary, an analyser and a speller from Giellatekno and Kvensk institutt, a rule-based Finnish to Kven model with 4,624 entries listed as a research project, and 55,506 tokens of Kven in Tartu's smugri4 data, but no public Norwegian to Kven translation service was found.
7. The National Library's Sami models are still in progress as of August 2026; its Borealis models are tuned for Bokmål, Nynorsk and English, and a May 2026 study of LLMs on North Sami reached chrF++ 17.79 into Sami without native-speaker validation.
8. Public practice is human translation: the Sami Parliament runs a full site in four languages; regjeringen.no has a North Sami section fed by a free translation service for the ministries; NRK Sápmi publishes in three Sami languages and Norwegian; UiT has Sami versions of selected unit pages; Nord and Statistics Norway have none.
9. Kven practice is thinner: NRK Kveeni has two journalists and publishes bilingually; Kvensk institutt publishes in Norwegian with Kven headings and translates on commission at a price set per job; the Kven museum's site offers Finnish but not Kven.
10. The government's own white paper says the obstacle is not technology but translators and a publishing strategy, and calls this costly; a 2011 to 2012 survey found 26 % of state sites with systematic Sami content and 5 % with a Sami version of the main content.

## 1. Law and policy

### The Language Act

The Act (lov om språk, 2021) states in § 5 "Samiske språk og norsk er likeverdige språk. Dei er jamstilte språk etter sameloven kapittel 3", and in § 6 "Kvensk, romani og romanes er nasjonale minoritetsspråk i Noreg". Section 9 adds that public bodies shall communicate in clear and correct language adapted to the target group. Public bodies (offentlege organ) are defined in § 2 to include state bodies, so a university falls under the Act. See [Lov om språk (språklova), Lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2021-05-21-42).

Språkrådet's guidance states that § 1 of the Act says public bodies shall protect and promote (verne og fremje) Sami and the national minority languages, and it spells out three duties on Sami: to use Sami inside the administrative area under the Sami Act; to develop Sami through active language care such as terminology; and to strengthen Sami, which "applies to all public bodies, not just those in the administrative area", with the listed examples being Sami language training, media content in Sami, "publishing websites in Sami languages", social media updates in Sami and Sami place names. See [Samiske språk, Språkrådet](https://sprakradet.no/spraklova/urfolkssprak-og-nasjonale-minoritetssprak/samiske-sprak/).

On the national minority languages, Språkrådet explains that protection means shielding the languages from lack of respect and sanctions, and promotion means supporting them "gjennom tiltak og handlinger", working for more use in society, and making it easier for the minorities to learn and use their language; examples given are a municipality publishing information videos in Romanes on its website, a directorate adopting a Kven name, and marking International Roma Day on a body's website or social media. The obligation applies to all public bodies, adapted to their area of responsibility. See [Nasjonale minoritetsspråk, Språkrådet](https://sprakradet.no/spraklova/urfolkssprak-og-nasjonale-minoritetssprak/nasjonale-minoritetssprak/) and [Verne og fremme, Språkrådet](https://sprakradet.no/spraklova/sporsmal-og-svar/spor-om-lov/verne-og-fremme/). Språkrådet's question and answer page on national minority languages contains no Kven-specific answers on websites or translation; see [Spørsmål og svar: nasjonale minoritetsspråk](https://sprakradet.no/spraklova/sporsmal-og-svar/sporsmal-og-svar-nasjonale-minoritetssprak/).

The bill behind the Act says of Kven that "Offentlege organ har eit ansvar for å verne og fremje kvensk" and that Kven "har like stor verdi som bruksspråk og kulturarv som norsk", but it establishes no individual right to receive information or services in Kven; the formal status mainly strengthens the implementation of the European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages. See [Prop. 108 L (2019–2020), chapter 13](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/prop.-108-l-20192020/id2701451/?ch=13).

### The Sami Act chapter 3

Chapter 3 defines the administrative area (forvaltningsområdet) and the public bodies bound by it. Section 3-2 requires ministries to translate laws and regulations of particular interest to the Sami population into Sami, and requires announcements and forms inside the area to appear in both Sami and Norwegian; in the text as fetched, national bodies are excluded from the form requirement. Section 3-3 gives a right to a written or oral reply in Sami from bodies inside the area, tiered by municipality type, and "landsomfattande organer" are exempt unless regulations say otherwise. Sections 3-4 to 3-6 cover courts, police, prisons, health and care institutions and the Church, again inside the area. Section 3-8 gives everyone a right to instruction in Sami, and § 3-12 makes the Sami Parliament responsible for protecting and developing Sami languages in Norway. See [Sameloven kapittel 3, Lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1987-06-12-56/KAPITTEL_3). Språkrådet states that only South, Lule and North Sami are covered by chapter 3; see [Samiske språk, Språkrådet](https://sprakradet.no/spraklova/urfolkssprak-og-nasjonale-minoritetssprak/samiske-sprak/).

The government's page, dated 2026-01-20, lists 14 municipalities in three tiers: language development municipalities (Kautokeino, Karasjok, Tana, Nesseby, Porsanger), language vitalisation municipalities (Kåfjord, Lavangen, Tjeldsund, Hamarøy, Hattfjelldal, Saltdal) and language stimulation municipalities (Snåsa, Røyrvik, Røros), plus the four county authorities of Finnmark, Troms, Nordland and Trøndelag. It states that the regulations "do not specify separate obligations for national state bodies". See [Forvaltningsområdet for samisk språk, regjeringen.no](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/tema/urfolk-og-minoriteter/samepolitikk/samiske-sprak/forvaltningsomradet-for-samisk-sprak/id2910947/). The Sami Parliament describes the same tiers and notes that municipalities can largely choose their own level and change category over time. See [Forvaltningsområdet for samiske språk, Sametinget](https://sametinget.no/sprak/forvaltningsomradet-for-samiske-sprak/).

What this means for a centre hosted at the University of Oslo: no provision of chapter 3 reaches it, since it is outside the area and a national body. The applicable duty is the general one in § 1 of the Language Act, which Språkrådet reads as a duty on every public body to strengthen Sami and to promote Kven through measures, of which a website in the language is one named example. Neither Språkrådet nor the government pages fetched turn that into a requirement to translate a website.

### Government policy on Sami on websites

The white paper on Sami language, culture and society reports a 2011 to 2012 survey by Difi of 234 state websites: 26 % published Sami content in a targeted and systematic way, 5 % had enough Sami content to count as a Sami version of the main Norwegian content, and 80 % had UTF-8 capability. It states that Sami languages are nearly absent on the sites of core state areas such as police, health, education and tax. It says that making Sami information available "er avhengig av oversettere og en strategi for publisering, og dette er kostbart", and that available content is often only in North Sami and hidden from users because pages lack a Sami language option. A Sami translation service was set up in the Departments' Security and Service Organisation (DSS) in 2010, funded by the ministry of local government, with framework agreements for North, South and Lule Sami, offering translation of press releases, news and articles to regjeringen.no at no cost to the ministries. See [Meld. St. 31 (2019–2020), chapter 3](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/meld.-st.-31-20192020/id2729805/?ch=3).

The Storting committee treating that paper wrote that the government should ensure "at det samiske perspektivet blir tatt inn tidlig i alt digitaliseringsarbeid" and that state bodies must give Sami speakers "likeverdig digitalt tilbud". See [Innst. 246 S (2020–2021)](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Publikasjoner/Innstillinger/Stortinget/2020-2021/inns-202021-246s/?all=true).

The government's page on Sami language technology says that all ICT development in the public sector shall build on universal design, so Sami characters must work in websites, user interfaces and registers; that Divvun is financed by the ministry of local government and its tools are free; and that Giellatekno has built a machine translation program from North Sami to Norwegian. See [Samisk språk og språkteknologi, regjeringen.no](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/tema/urfolk-og-minoriteter/samepolitikk/samiske-sprak/samisk-sprak-og-it/id86947/). An older English page says the government's Sami web pages were established in 2003 as "the main channel for information from the Government and the Ministries in Sámi" and that central agencies shall assess the need for Sami support in their systems. See [The use of the Sami language in the public administration](https://www.regjeringen.no/en/topics/indigenous-peoples-and-minorities/Sami-people/samiske-sprak/the-use-of-the-sami-language-in-the-publ/id86942/).

### The universities with Sami and Kven responsibility

UiT's language policy states "UiT har et nasjonalt ansvar for nordsamisk og kvensk" and "UiT skal legge til rette for at disse språkene kan høres og ses ved institusjonen". On websites it says "UiT skal etterstrebe å ha sentrale dokumenter og nettsider av særlig viktighet for organisasjonen tilgjengelig i parallellutgave oversatt til nordsamisk", that units shall have names in Nynorsk, Bokmål, Sami, Kven and English, and that North Sami enquiries should be answered in North Sami. See [Språkpolitiske retningslinjer ved UiT](https://uit.no/utdanning/art?p_document_id=347818&dim=179017). UiT offers a year course, a bachelor and a master in Kven and Finnish; see [Kvensk og finsk, master, UiT](https://uit.no/utdanning/program/610035/kvensk_og_finsk_-_master).

Nord University states a national responsibility for research and education in Lule and South Sami language and culture, with three Sami language programmes from autumn 2023 and a Centre for Sami and Indigenous Studies established in 2022. See [Tre nye samiske språkstudier fra høsten 2023, Nord](https://www.nord.no/en/node/35454).

Sámi allaskuvla's site is offered in North Sami as the default, with English and Norwegian versions; no written language policy was found on the front page. See [samas.no](https://samas.no/).

### The Sami Parliament, Språkrådet and Kvensk institutt

The Sami Parliament's language technology page states that "De samiske språkene trenger språkbrukere, og aktive språkbrukere behøver moderne hjelpemidler for data, nett og mobile enheter" and that the council works with research and development actors on all Sami languages; it contains no statement on machine translation. See [Språkteknologi, Sametinget](https://sametinget.no/sprak/sprakteknologi/). A page titled "Retningslinjer for bruk av kunstig intelligens i Sametinget" exists but its body could not be read; see [Retningslinjer for bruk av kunstig intelligens i Sametinget](https://sametinget.no/om-sametinget/administrasjonen/retningslinjer-for-bruk-av-kunstig-intelligens-i-sametinget/). [The page should be checked in a browser before the plan is finalised.]

Kvensk institutt is the national centre for Kven language and culture. It translates texts "innenfor de fleste fagområder, fra pressemeldinger til læremidler" from Norwegian to Kven and proofreads Kven texts, with prices depending on length, content and deadline; the page gives no advice on machine translation. See [Oversettelser til kvensk, Kvensk institutt](https://www.kvenskinstitutt.no/om-kainun-institutti/oversettelser-til-kvensk/). The institute runs a language council and a language assembly (Kvensk språkråd and Kvensk språkting) for standardisation, and notes that Kven was recognised as a language in April 2005. See [Språk, Kvensk institutt](https://www.kvenskinstitutt.no/sprak/). The teaching resource site kvensk.no is a joint project of Kvensk institutt, Språkrådet and the Directorate for Education, and runs bilingually in Kven and Norwegian. See [kvensk.no](https://www.kvensk.no/).

## 2. Technology

### Divvun and Giellatekno (UiT)

Divvun describes its tools as free, mostly open source and available to all: Divvun Manager for spellers, keyboards and text-to-speech on Windows and macOS, a grammar checker for Word and Google Docs, a mobile keyboard with spelling, and an online speller, for North, South, Lule, Inari and Skolt Sami. See [Divvun](https://divvun.no/no/). Divvun's translation page links only two resources: machine translation at gtweb.uit.no/jorgal and a Norwegian to North Sami translation memory as a TMX download. See [Oversettelse, Divvun](https://divvun.no/no/oversettelse/oversettelse.html).

Giellatekno's overview lists machine translation "fra nordsamisk til norsk" at jorgal.uit.no and beta versions "mellom samiske språk (nordsamisk <-> lule og enaresamisk)" at gtweb.uit.no/mt, marked as experimental. See [Verktøy for samiske språk, Giellatekno](https://giellatekno.uit.no/smilang.nob.html).

Jorgal states: "The North Saami-Norwegian translation system is under constant development. In its present phase it may be used to understand North Saami better, but it is not able to produce flawless Norwegian." It says the team does not offer Norwegian to North Sami because "Norwegian and Saami are very different languages, and the quality of such a translated text would be far from what one could use as a publishable translation", with concern that poor output could damage the North Sami written language. See [Jorgal, Giellatekno and Apertium](https://gtweb.uit.no/jorgal). NRK reported in February 2017 that the translator goes one way only, a choice Giellatekno made "to secure that this will not be misused". See [Nordsamisk får sin egen «Google Translate», NRK Sápmi](https://www.nrk.no/sapmi/nordsamisk-far-sin-egen-_google-translate_-1.13380940).

An evaluation by Trosterud and Antonsen (2021) of the rule-based North Sami to Norwegian system over two domains found that lexical errors are the most harmful for comprehension while grammatical and pragmatic errors lower fluency without much effect on understanding. See [Hva er viktig for forståelse? Oslo Studies in Language](https://journals.uio.no/osla/article/view/8514).

### Apertium pairs

- sme-nob: North Sami to Bokmål only, GPL-2.0, built on the giella-sme analyser and apertium-nob, with regression tests and evaluation data. See [apertium-sme-nob, GitHub](https://github.com/apertium/apertium-sme-nob). The Apertium wiki confirms a single direction and lists open issues on definiteness, the auxiliary "leat" and compounds. See [Northern Sámi and Norwegian, Apertium wiki](https://wiki.apertium.org/wiki/Northern_S%C3%A1mi_and_Norwegian). The pair is packaged in Debian as apertium-sme-nob 0.6.1+ds.1-2, dated February 2021, and in Ubuntu 22.04 and 24.04 (universe). See [Debian pool, apertium-sme-nob](https://mirrors.mit.edu/debian/pool/main/a/apertium-sme-nob) and [Ubuntu packages search](https://packages.ubuntu.com/search?keywords=apertium-sme-nob). Newer builds come from Apertium's nightly repository, installed with a script. See [apt-install-nightly.sh, apertium/packaging](https://github.com/apertium/packaging/blob/main/scripts/apt-install-nightly.sh). In a GitHub Actions job on ubuntu-latest, apt can install it as the site already installs Apertium for Nynorsk; the direction is the wrong one for a mirror.
- smj-nob: Lule Sami and Bokmål, GPL-3.0, incubator status, 25 commits, modes for both directions but the README test sentence is still "TODO". See [apertium-smj-nob, GitHub](https://github.com/apertium/apertium-smj-nob).
- sme-smj: North and Lule Sami, both directions, GPL-2.0, 2,161 commits. See [apertium-sme-smj, GitHub](https://github.com/apertium/apertium-sme-smj).
- sme-sma: North and South Sami, both directions, GPL-3.0, nursery status, 2,395 commits. See [apertium-sme-sma, GitHub](https://github.com/apertium/apertium-sme-sma).
- Kven: no Apertium fin-fkv or fkv-nob repository was found. A 2025 survey by Pirinen, Trosterud and Rueter lists a rule-based Finnish to Kven model with 4,624 entries in its table of machine translation models, a set whose range is "from usable machine translators to early stage projects", and notes that Kven has one grammatical model, a keyboard, and text collections beyond Wikipedia, while "the basis for data-driven language technology is shaky". See [Language technology for the minority Finnic languages, IWCLUL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.iwclul-1.6.pdf).

### Kven tools

The GiellaLT lang-fkv repository provides morphological analysers and proofing tools for Kven under LGPL-3.0, marked production ready, with speller files installed through the Divvun Installer and a separate keyboard project; it mentions no machine translation. See [giellalt/lang-fkv, GitHub](https://github.com/giellalt/lang-fkv). Kvensk institutt's language technology project with Giellatekno began in January 2013 and has produced a dictionary with inflection lookup, an analyser, a speller and learning tools; its page says nothing on machine translation. See [Kvensk språkteknologiprosjekt, Kvensk institutt](https://www.kvenskinstitutt.no/eget-sprakarbeid/kvensk-sprakteknologiprosjekt/). NRK Kveeni reported in September 2022 that Kven has an electronic dictionary and a speller, with Trosterud quoted that not all languages have access to all the tools. See [Unike språkverktøy brukes for å styrke små nordiske språk, NRK Kveeni](https://www.nrk.no/kvensk/unike-sprakverktoy-brukes-for-a-styrke-sma-nordiske-sprak-1.16094738).

### TartuNLP (Neurotõlge, smugri)

The smugri3 model translates in 702 directions between 27 languages: Estonian, English, Finnish, Hungarian, Latvian, Norwegian and Russian, and 20 low-resource Finno-Ugric languages including Northern, Southern, Inari, Lule and Skolt Sami. Licence CC-BY-4.0. Kven is not in the model. See [tartuNLP/smugri3-finno-ugric-nmt, Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/tartuNLP/smugri3-finno-ugric-nmt). The smugri4 training data does include Kven (fkv, 55,506 tokens) alongside Norwegian, under mixed Creative Commons terms with the repository marked CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0. See [tartuNLP/smugri4-data, Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/tartuNLP/smugri4-data).

The public demo is translate.ut.ee; the University of Tartu's announcement (March 2023) quotes its research fellow that "translation quality can vary a lot, and it can be especially low when translating into low-resourced languages", and describes the engine as open source and the first public support for most of these languages. See [University of Tartu machine translation engine now supports 17 new Finno-Ugric languages](https://ut.ee/en/content/university-tartu-machine-translation-engine-now-supports-17-new-finno-ugric-languages). NRK Sápmi covered the same launch, noting translation between six Sami languages and to and from English and Norwegian. See [Nye muligheter for å oversette mellom samiske språk, NRK Sápmi](https://www.nrk.no/sapmi/nye-muligheter-for-a-oversette-mellom-samiske-sprak-1.16355717). A LUMI article from June 2024 repeats the quality warning and says the purpose is to let philologists and others understand texts, and to support speakers by translating into the languages. See [Preserving endangered languages with LUMI](https://lumi-supercomputer.eu/preserving-endangered-languages/).

The 2023 paper reports an average chrF++ of 26.8 from high-resource to low-resource languages on their FLORES extension, and on an earlier test set BLEU of 35.21 for Norwegian to North Sami, 60.79 for Norwegian to South Sami and 51.95 for Norwegian to Lule Sami. See [Machine Translation for Low-resource Finno-Ugric Languages, NoDaLiDa 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.nodalida-1.77.pdf). [The South and Lule figures are much higher than the North Sami one and come from a small test set of the authors' own; they should not be read as a quality ranking without a native reader.]

Access: a hosted REST API is documented at api.tartunlp.ai/translation/docs, with no rate limit or terms stated in the repository, a default input limit of 10,000 characters, and Docker self-hosting. See [TartuNLP/translation-api, GitHub](https://github.com/TartuNLP/translation-api). The worker that serves the models is MIT licensed, runs from a Docker image, and needs about 8 GB of GPU memory, with CPU use possible but slower. See [TartuNLP/translation-worker, GitHub](https://github.com/TartuNLP/translation-worker). A GitHub Actions build could call the hosted API or, with more effort, run the CC-BY model on CPU via transformers; neither the API's terms of use nor its uptime are documented on the pages fetched.

### The National Library

The AI lab has released OCR models for Sami under CC BY 4.0. See [OCR-modeller for samiske språk, README](https://www.nb.no/sbfil/samisk_ocr/README_no.pdf). The Borealis model family is tuned for Bokmål, Nynorsk and English; the page says only that NorMistral "remains very strong on some Sámi and translation tasks", with no Sami evaluation for Borealis. See [Borealis, AI-lab](https://ai.nb.no/borealis/). The press release of 10 August 2026 on the new models states "Arbeidet med samiske språkmodeller pågår". See [Nye språkmodeller fra Nasjonalbiblioteket](https://www.nb.no/pressemeldinger/nye-sprakmodeller-fra-nasjonalbiblioteket-skal-styrke-ki-tilpasset-norske-forhold/). The 2025 budget set aside 40 million kroner for training and releasing Norwegian and Sami language models, 20 million to the National Library and 20 million to Sigma2 for compute. See [40 millionar til styrking av norsk og samisk språk i KI](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/40-millionar-til-styrking-av-norsk-og-samisk-sprak-i-ki/id3056102/).

### Large language models on North Sami

A study by Einar Holt (Tenki, May 2026, version 0.2) on a consumer GPU reports chrF++ 23.96 for North Sami to Norwegian and chrF++ 17.79 for Norwegian to North Sami with 45.5 % of outputs accepted by the Apertium morphology; it was done without collaboration with the Sami Parliament or native-speaker validation, and argues for a "teach-don't-speak" deployment where every Sami output comes from the rule-based tools rather than the model. See [LLMs for North Sami, Tenki](https://tenki.no/forskning/llms-for-north-sami-multitrack-empirical-study/pdf?download=1).

### Terminology

The sátni service exposes a REST API at satni.uit.no/satnibackend with dictionaries across North, South, Lule, Inari and Skolt Sami and pairs with Finnish, Norwegian, Estonian, Swedish and Kven; the termwiki content is "based on Sametinget's term collection", and most dictionaries carry an unspecified copyright. See [Sátni REST end points, GiellaLT](https://giellalt.uit.no/apps/satni/RESTEndPoints.html). The satni.org front page returned no readable content when fetched.

### What the institutions say about machine translation for public information

Giellatekno's own statement is the clearest: the North Sami to Norwegian system is for understanding, not for publishable Norwegian, and the reverse direction is withheld because the output would be far from publishable and could harm the language ([Jorgal](https://gtweb.uit.no/jorgal)). The Sami Parliament's language technology page contains no position on machine translation ([Språkteknologi, Sametinget](https://sametinget.no/sprak/sprakteknologi/)), and the government's page presents Giellatekno's translator as a tool that lets Sami institutions use North Sami internally ([Samisk språk og språkteknologi](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/tema/urfolk-og-minoriteter/samepolitikk/samiske-sprak/samisk-sprak-og-it/id86947/)). No page from Kvensk institutt mentions machine translation.

## 3. Practice

Language markers below come from fetching the front pages and reading their language links and lang attributes on 2026-09-17.

- Sami Parliament (sametinget.no): a full site in Norwegian, North Sami, Lule Sami and South Sami, with a language switch in the main navigation. See [Sametinget](https://sametinget.no/). This is human translation; the site is the body responsible for Sami under § 3-12.
- regjeringen.no: a North Sami section at /se/ with links to the Norwegian and English versions and no Lule or South Sami alternative, and no statement on scope or method on the page itself. See [regjeringen.no, sámegillii](https://www.regjeringen.no/se/id4/). The white paper explains the supply chain: DSS translates press releases, news and articles free of charge to the ministries with framework agreements in three Sami languages, and every ministry has basic information in North Sami. See [Meld. St. 31 (2019–2020), chapter 3](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/meld.-st.-31-20192020/id2729805/?ch=3).
- NRK Sápmi: publishes "på fire språk; nord-, sør- og lulesamisk og norsk", with most broadcasting in North Sami and regular Lule and South Sami programmes; it is the smallest content division in NRK. See [Om NRK Sápmi](https://www.nrk.no/sapmi/om/1.-om-nrk-sapmi-1.11275408). The front page carries a North Sami news section and articles marked with lang="sme" and lang="smj", with Norwegian versions alongside. See [NRK Sápmi](https://www.nrk.no/sapmi/). This is original journalism in the languages rather than translation of a Norwegian site.
- UiT: the site framework offers Norsk, English and Sámegillii on selected unit pages, for example the Sami law research page, whose Sami version sits beside Norwegian and English URLs. See [Sámegillii, UiT urfolksrett](https://uit.no/research/urfolksrett_sa). The policy commitment is to parallel North Sami editions of central documents and pages of particular importance, not a mirror ([Språkpolitiske retningslinjer ved UiT](https://uit.no/utdanning/art?p_document_id=347818&dim=179017)). No Kven version of any UiT page was found.
- Nord University: the site offers Norwegian Bokmål and English only, with no Lule or South Sami version. See [Nord universitet](https://www.nord.no/).
- Sámi allaskuvla: North Sami default with English and Norwegian. See [samas.no](https://samas.no/).
- Statistics Norway: the Sami statistics page is in Norwegian only, with no Sami language option. See [Samiske forhold, SSB](https://www.ssb.no/befolkning/statistikker/samisk/hvert-2-aar).
- NRK Kveeni: bilingual Kven and Norwegian content, including the podcast Kaffipraatti described as the first in Kven, produced by two journalists. See [NRK Kvensk](https://www.nrk.no/kvensk/).
- Kvensk institutt: the site is marked lang="nb-NO" with Kven headings (Kvääni) throughout; translation is a commissioned service priced per job. See [Kvensk institutt](https://www.kvenskinstitutt.no/) and [Oversettelser til kvensk](https://www.kvenskinstitutt.no/om-kainun-institutti/oversettelser-til-kvensk/).
- Varanger museum (Vadsø museum, Ruija kvenmuseum): the site offers Norwegian, English, German, Russian and Finnish, with no Kven option; Kven appears in exhibition titles. See [Varanger museum](https://www.varangermuseum.no/).
- kvensk.no: parallel Kven and Norwegian throughout, as a teaching resource. See [kvensk.no](https://www.kvensk.no/).

Marking: none of the Sami sites fetched labels content as machine translated, because none uses it; the government and Sami Parliament sites use language switches and hreflang or lang attributes (se-NO on regjeringen.no, sme and smj on NRK). Cost and capacity figures found: DSS translation is free to the ministries and covers three Sami languages ([Meld. ## 4. Options for MishMash

The site today has English, Bokmål and a Nynorsk mirror generated at build time with Apertium. The options below differ in what is promised, who checks the text, and whether the build touches Sami or Kven at all. None is recommended here.

### Option A. Selected pages in North Sami and Kven, translated by people

Translate a fixed small set (front page, about, contact, one page per programme) into North Sami and Kven, commissioned from a Sami translator and from Kvensk institutt, and add a language switch and hreflang for those pages only. This matches UiT's own policy of parallel editions of central pages ([UiT guidelines](https://uit.no/utdanning/art?p_document_id=347818&dim=179017)) and Språkrådet's example of a website in the language as promotion ([Samiske språk, Språkrådet](https://sprakradet.no/spraklova/urfolkssprak-og-nasjonale-minoritetssprak/samiske-sprak/)). Trade-offs: cost per page and per update, drift between versions as the Norwegian pages change, and a choice of North Sami alone or all three Sami languages, which the government notes is the usual pattern and the usual criticism ([Meld. St. 31](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/meld.-st.-31-20192020/id2729805/?ch=3)).

### Option B. Full mirror in North Sami by machine, reviewed before publication

Run the Bokmål pages through TartuNLP's API or self-hosted model in the build, hold the output in a review branch, and publish only pages a reader has approved. The model covers Norwegian to North, Lule and South Sami under CC-BY-4.0 ([smugri3](https://huggingface.co/tartuNLP/smugri3-finno-ugric-nmt)) with an API and Docker worker ([translation-api](https://github.com/TartuNLP/translation-api), [translation-worker](https://github.com/TartuNLP/translation-worker)). Trade-offs: the developers warn that quality can be especially low into low-resource languages ([University of Tartu](https://ut.ee/en/content/university-tartu-machine-translation-engine-now-supports-17-new-finno-ugric-languages)); Giellatekno refuses to publish the same direction on principle ([Jorgal](https://gtweb.uit.no/jorgal)); Kven is not in the model at all; the API has no published terms or uptime; and the review load is the whole site, which reintroduces the cost the option was meant to avoid.

### Option C. Unreviewed machine mirror, clearly labelled, as for Nynorsk

Treat Sami like Nynorsk: generate at build time and label every page as machine translated. Trade-offs: Nynorsk is a written standard of the same language and has a mature Apertium pair, while Norwegian to Sami has no rule-based pair and the only neural system carries its authors' warning; the body that builds Sami language technology has stated that unedited output in this direction "would be far from what one could use as a publishable translation" and could harm the written language ([Jorgal](https://gtweb.uit.no/jorgal)); no public Sami site fetched publishes machine output; and there is nothing to generate for Kven. The option is cheap and complete on paper and carries reputational risk with the community whose language it is meant to promote.

### Option D. Promotion without translation of the site

Meet the § 1 duty through other named measures: Sami and Kven names for the centre and its programmes, Sami place names, a statement in North Sami and Kven on the front page, Sami and Kven material in events and blog posts, and correct lang attributes and character support. Språkrådet lists names, social media updates and place names among the measures ([Samiske språk](https://sprakradet.no/spraklova/urfolkssprak-og-nasjonale-minoritetssprak/samiske-sprak/), [Nasjonale minoritetsspråk](https://sprakradet.no/spraklova/urfolkssprak-og-nasjonale-minoritetssprak/nasjonale-minoritetssprak/)), and the government requires Sami characters to work in public web systems ([Samisk språk og språkteknologi](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/tema/urfolk-og-minoriteter/samepolitikk/samiske-sprak/samisk-sprak-og-it/id86947/)). Trade-offs: no Sami or Kven reader gets the content of the site, and the measure may read as symbolic; it costs little and can be kept current.

### Option E. Option A plus a machine-assisted workflow for the translator

Commission human translation of selected pages, but give the translator TartuNLP output, the Divvun grammar checker and the sátni terminology API as tools, and keep a translation memory in the repository so that updates are cheaper than first translations. Divvun already publishes a Norwegian to North Sami translation memory and a grammar checker for Word and Google Docs ([Oversettelse, Divvun](https://divvun.no/no/oversettelse/oversettelse.html), [Divvun](https://divvun.no/no/)); sátni has a REST API ([Sátni REST end points](https://giellalt.uit.no/apps/satni/RESTEndPoints.html)); for Kven there is a speller and dictionary but no translation model, so that side remains fully manual ([lang-fkv](https://github.com/giellalt/lang-fkv)). Trade-offs: more setup than A, the same dependence on a translator's availability, and a workflow that a research centre on AI and creativity could document as a case study, which is also a reason to be careful about being seen to test tools on the language rather than serve its readers.
