On 14 and 15 September, MishMash held its [opening conference](/events/kristiansand2026/) in Kristiansand. The two days moved between Kilden Performing Arts Centre, the Kunstsilo museum and the Kraftverk venue, and gathered researchers from the partner institutions with artists, students and people from the cultural and creative sector.

## Monday: keynote, tracks and posters

Sunniva Whittaker, rector of the University of Agder, Anne Kjersti Fahlvik from the Research Council of Norway and MishMash director Alexander Refsum Jensenius opened the conference. Then, Jane C. Ginsburg, professor of literary and artistic property law at Columbia University, gave the first keynote on copyright, artistic creation and artificial intelligence.

The rest of the day followed the three perspectives of the centre&mdash;humans, machines and society&mdash;moderated by centre directors Alexander Refsum Jensenius, Ida Jahr, and Daniel Nordgård. Over lunch, 42 posters were presented in eight thematic clusters, from human and AI interaction to responsible AI and critical futures. In the afternoon, seven parallel sessions covered creative performance with AI, AI in artistic processes, health, game development education, the creative value chain, cultural heritage, and collaboration in education. Makers from music, film and museums then reflected on the importance of knowledge when using AI in creative processes, before Jane Ginsburg, Jon McCormack and Anne Kjersti Fahlvik set the agenda for the near future in the closing session.

The evening continued at Kraftverk/BARE with MishMash LIVE, a mini festival with local food, art installations, and short talks with the creators of the film Julius, Kunstsilo's project From Artwork to Algorithm and the sound installation [*The Logos*](https://thelogos.art).

{% include photo-carousel.html name="bare" %}

The University of Agder made a short film from each day. In the first, deputy director Daniel Nordgård describes MishMash as a mashup of many approaches to the challenge and potential of AI, and a network of academic and non-academic partners working on fair, responsible and artistic uses of it.

## Tuesday: together with Tech:Art

The second day was organised together with Tech:Art at Kilden and turned towards the cultural and creative sector. Gerfried Stocker of Ars Electronica gave the opening keynote, followed by NRK on the use of new technologies in cultural practice and a dialogue on research in practice from MishMash. Jane Ginsburg returned with an expert statement on rights and ownership in artistic work. 

After lunch, Kilden, Det Norske Teatret, Piksel and other Norwegian cultural organisations presented their work with new technology, Sarah Ellis of the Royal Shakespeare Company gave the second keynote, and industry representatives discussed their use of new technologies. MishMash Scientific Advisory Board member Jon McCormack of Monash University closed the programme with insights and musings on artistic co-creation with frontier AI models.

{% include photo-carousel.html name="kilden-tuesday" %}

The conference ended in the concrete silos of Kunstsilo, where Alessandra Bossa performed .*skin*, an installation-performance for three musicians, steel membranes, flexible mirrors, transducers and machine listening, followed by a party on the roof.

{% include photo-carousel.html name="kunstsilo" %}

In the film from the second day, centre director Alexander Refsum Jensenius points out that AI is not new to the arts: artistic exploration has been part of its development from the beginning. Conference chair Roderick Udo explains why shared experiences, such as the evening at Kunstsilo, are where connections are made.

The abstracts of all sessions and posters are collected in the [book of abstracts](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23009316), published on Zenodo. The [next MishMash conference](/events/kristiania2027/) takes place in Oslo in April 2027, hosted  by Kristiania University College.
