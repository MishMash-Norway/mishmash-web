summary: 'I am Professor of Popular Music Studies at the University of Oslo. My research
  combines popular music analysis with cultural studies and media theory, with particular
  emphasis on music production, sound, rhythm, sampling, and music technologies.


  I am involved in the Norwegian research centres MishMash – Centre for AI and Creativity;
  RITMO – Centre for Interdisciplinary Studies in Rhythm, Time and Motion; and CreaTeME
  – Creative use of Technologies in Music Education. I am a Visiting Professor at
  the Centre for Research in Music and its Technologies at the University of Huddersfield
  and previously held an adjunct professorship at the University of Agder. I have
  also been a visiting scholar at University of Edinburgh, Columbia University, and
  American University. I was involved in the founding of SPMR (Society for Music Production
  Research), where I currently serve as assistant editor of its journal.


  My research has focused on how production technologies leave audible traces in music
  through fragmentation, glitches, samples, manipulated voices, and other forms of
  technologically mediated sound. I have been particularly interested in remix culture,
  platform governance, content moderation, copyright, and authorship – an interest
  that informed the Research Council of Norway funded project MASHED: Mashup Music,
  Copyright, and Platform Regulation, which I led from 2018–2021. I have also worked
  extensively on rhythm, timing, and sound through the RCN-funded projects TIME: Timing
  and Sound in Musical Microrhythm (Toppforsk; 2016–2023), and Rhythm in the Age of
  Digital Reproduction (FRIPRO; 2004–2010), both led by Anne Danielsen. More recently,
  my research has expanded to questions related to AI and the sustainability of music
  cultures and industries.


  I am the author of the monographs Parody in the Age of Remix: Mashup Creativity
  vs. the Takedown (2023) and Digital Signatures: The Impact of Digitization on Popular
  Music Sound (with Anne Danielsen, 2016), both published by MIT Press. I am currently
  co-editing The Oxford Handbook of Sampling (with A. Danielsen, P. Harkins, and N.
  Prior; forthcoming 2027). My research has appeared in journals including Popular
  Music, New Media & Society, Popular Music & Society, Music Theory Spectrum, Music
  Theory Online, Music Perception, Organised Sound, and The Journal of Music Production
  Research.


  I have supervised numerous Master’s students, 6 PhD candidates, and 6 postdoctoral
  researchers, including Marie Skłodowska-Curie-funded postdoctoral research (ERC).
  I have also been engaged in research training and career development initiatives
  at the University of Oslo. My teaching covers popular music studies, music technologies
  and their musical and cultural impact, sound- and groove-based music analysis, music
  and media, and research methodology.


  Before becoming Professor at the University of Oslo in 2021, I completed my PhD
  there in 2013 and was hired as Associate Professor in 2015 after a short postdoctoral
  period. Alongside my academic work, I remain engaged in music production and other
  forms of artistic practice.



  Please visit my personal research website at www.ragnhildbrovig.com'
