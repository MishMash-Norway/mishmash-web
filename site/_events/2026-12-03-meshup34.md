This week, [Emilio Ruiz](https://www.simula.no/people/emilio), research scientist in the SIGIPRO Department at [Simula Metropolitan](/institutions/simula-metropolitan-center-for-digital-engineering/), will present *On the Limitations of Hand Trajectory Prediction from Movement Sensors*.

## Abstract

Preserving a continuous and immersive sense of embodiment is critical for human operators in robot arm teleoperation systems. However, this embodied state is frequently disrupted by inherent network and system delays, which sever the perceptual link between the operator and the robotic arms. To address this challenge, we are testing predictive delay compensation to hide latency by continuously anticipating the operator's movements and feedback. Currently, we are investigating the limitations of existing forecasting models, specifically regarding their predictive accuracy and delay compensation capabilities, when relying solely on kinematic movement sensors. To overcome these limitations, our ongoing research focuses on integrating multimodal physiological signals, such as brain and muscle activity, into the forecasting process. This research is part of our broader effort to design machine learning and control algorithms that enable remote, bidirectional physical interactions (both forward and feedback prediction for robotic control) across a range of applications, such as physical rehabilitation and collaborative physical work, among others.
