This week, [Lyuba Boncheva](/people/lyuba-boncheva/) presents *Value creation processes in tech-culture partnerships: a case study of the partnership between Det Norske Teatret and NetNordic*, work written with Anne-Britt Gran at BI Norwegian Business School.

## Abstract

AI technologies are changing the conditions of cultural production, but what a partnership between a technology company and a cultural institution does to value creation is still little studied. The paper approaches this through the collaboration between Det Norske Teatret and the technology provider NetNordic, which produced the play *Prosessen mot menneska*, The Trial Against Humanity.

Set in a dystopian courtroom, the play gives the audience a last chance to defend humanity against the AI character Omnitron, with audience interaction feeding the theatre's AI-driven character in real time. That makes it an unusually clear case for studying how value is created around AI in cultural production.

The paper asks how value creation is affected when cultural institutions partner with technology companies to produce AI-mediated audience experiences, and looks at three parties in turn: the cultural institution, the technology company and the audience. It draws on theories of cultural value, on value co-creation, and on the concepts of deterritorialisation and reterritorialisation from assemblage theory.

The talk presents the research design and the state of the data collection, and discusses early directions on the audience question from an ongoing analysis of 15 qualitative audience interviews. The work is part of the creAIte project, funded by the Research Council of Norway.

## Bio

Lyuba Boncheva is a second-year doctoral fellow on the creAIte project at the Centre for Creative Industries, BI Norwegian Business School. She holds a bachelor's and a master's degree in communication and media studies from Leipzig University, with a minor in museum studies, and a master of philosophy in screen cultures from the University of Oslo.

Her research is on how transformative technologies such as AI affect value creation for cultural institutions and their audiences, and in particular on technology as a mediator of cultural experience. She comes from work in art centres and museums, and is interested in keeping academic research and practice within reach of each other. She is a member of work packages 5 and 6.
