This week, [Lyuba Boncheva](/people/lyuba-boncheva/) presents *Value creation processes in tech-culture partnerships: a case study of the partnership between Det Norske Teatret and NetNordic*, work written with Anne-Britt Gran at BI Norwegian Business School.

## Abstract

What happens to value creation when a theatre and a technology company make a performance together, with an AI system on stage? Lyuba Boncheva takes that question to the collaboration between Det Norske Teatret and the technology provider NetNordic, which produced the play *Prosessen mot menneska*, The Trial Against Humanity. Set in a dystopian courtroom, the play gives the audience a last chance to defend humanity against the AI character Omnitron, and what the audience does feeds the theatre's AI-driven character in real time.

The talk follows value creation for three parties in turn, the cultural institution, the technology company and the audience, and draws on theories of cultural value, on value co-creation, and on deterritorialisation and reterritorialisation from assemblage theory. It is work in progress: the presentation lays out the research design, says where the data collection stands, and opens the first directions on the audience question from an ongoing analysis of 15 qualitative audience interviews. The work is written with Anne-Britt Gran and is part of the [creAIte project](https://www.bi.no/en/research/research-centres/centre-for-creative-industries/creaite/) at BI Norwegian Business School, funded by the Research Council of Norway.

## Bio

Lyuba Boncheva is a second-year doctoral fellow on the [creAIte project](https://www.bi.no/en/research/research-centres/centre-for-creative-industries/creaite/) at the Centre for Creative Industries, BI Norwegian Business School, where she studies how transformative technologies such as AI affect value creation for cultural institutions and their audiences, and in particular how technology mediates a cultural experience. She holds a bachelor's and a master's degree in communication and media studies from Leipzig University, with a minor in museum studies, and a master of philosophy in screen cultures from the University of Oslo. She comes from work in art centres and museums, keeps academic research and practice within reach of each other, and is a member of work packages 5 and 6.
