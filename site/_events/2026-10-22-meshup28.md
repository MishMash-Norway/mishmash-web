This week, [Lise Amy Hansen](/people/lise-amy-hansen/) and Roos van Berkel from the Oslo School of Architecture and Design (AHO) will present *Making Movement Matter*.

## Abstract

Movement is how we sense, express ourselves and connect with others and the world. As AI tools for capturing, analysing and responding to movement develop quickly, it matters what systems treat as movement in the first place: what they track, what they shape, and what they quietly discard.

In this MeshUp, Lise Amy Hansen will start by introducing [*Making Movement Matter*](/projects/making-movement-matter/), the overarching theme of the research group ENACT at AHO. It focuses on how the kinaesthetic and relational qualities of movement, beyond position and poses, can be made palpable and accessible for the development of systems where movement is tracked.

PhD Fellow Roos van Berkel will briefly discuss her research *Drifting Bodies through Algorithms*, which turns the logic of tracking around. With flocking algorithms, we developed a participatory, choreographic score that experiments with real-time motion tracking as a relational technology. The scores explore different layers of attention regarding the self and surrounding entities. Participants move between their own attention, each other and live visualisations, and the work asks where agency sits when bodies and algorithms move together.

## The (UN)TRACKABLE symposium

This talk also previews *(UN)TRACKABLE: Movement Data and AI*, a two-day symposium at AHO on Thursday 29 and Friday 30 October 2026, hosted by the research group ENACT. We would love to see many of you there. The symposium brings together scholars and practitioners from design, choreography, movement studies, HCI, philosophy and disability studies. Together we will look critically and constructively at how AI-driven tools track, shape or discard movement, with attention to agency, inclusion, plurality and individuation. Over two days of talks, movement sessions, readings and round tables, contributors include Anton Koch and David Ritterhaus (Motion Bank, Mainz), Sarah Kettley (Edinburgh College of Art), Jan Grue (UiO), Christoph Hubatschke (IT:U), Sofie Wass and Leona Chandra Kruse (UiA), Asaf Bachrach (CNRS) and Bojana Cvejić (KHiO).

MishMashers are especially welcome at the MishMash WP1 breakfast session on Thursday 29 October from 9:30 to 11:00, ahead of the official opening at 12:00. The venue is the AHO Gallery (ground floor, wheelchair accessible).

## Bios

Lise Amy Hansen is a Professor and currently the Research Chair at the Institute of Design, AHO. She leads the research group ENACT, which focuses on designing for real-time experiences. Her research interest is in how performance and technology meet, with a particular focus on body movement practices and their materialisations, relations, aesthetics and ethics. Current projects include Making Movement Matter (see above) and the Horizon project (Un)EXTRA, on how design can strengthen Europe's resilience in a time of resource scarcity, extreme conditions and posthuman transition.

Roos van Berkel is a dancer, choreographer and movement analyst. Currently, she is a PhD Fellow at the Design Institute of AHO and a part-time lecturer at the department Expanded Contemporary Dance at the Amsterdam University of the Arts. Her interdisciplinary artistic practice focuses on patterns, scores and technology.
