This week, [Ole-Andreas Rognstad](/people/ole-andreas-rognstad/) from the [Department of Private Law](https://www.jus.uio.no/ifp/english/) at the University of Oslo presents *The Mixed Ideological Underpinnings of Copyright Law in the AI Age*.

## Abstract

The presentation explores how copyright law’s underlying ideology is not singular or fully coherent, but instead “mixed”. This is a feature that becomes especially visible in technologically complex and politically charged contexts, like generative AI. Drawing on the role of purpose in legal interpretation (not least in international and EU law), the speaker argues that the stated objectives of copyright are often concealed in legal argumentation. The current discussion about the use of copyright protected works in the development and dissemination of generative AI models and systems is illustrative.

Because copyright law must reconcile multiple, sometimes competing rationales, ranging from utilitarian incentive and reward theories to Lockean labor/property intuitions and Kantian or personality-based ideas, as well as more public-discourse or humanitarian approaches, the legal doctrine can conceal normative choices behind doctrinal and technological reasoning. The presentation proposes that legal scholarship and jurisprudence would benefit from greater clarity about copyright’s purpose and the political premises underlying doctrinal outcomes, not least when it comes to AI regulation.

## Bio

[Ole-Andreas Rognstad](https://www.jus.uio.no/ifp/personer/vit/olearogn/) is Professor of Law at the Department of Private Law, University of Oslo. He teaches a whole range of subjects, including legal methodology, EU/EEA law and intellectual property law, in particular copyright law. His authorship includes a textbook on (Norwegian) copyright law, a monograph on Property Aspects of Intellectual Property as well as a large number of articles mainly in the field of intellectual property, including on copyright and AI. He has chaired, and been a member of, a number of public dispute settlement resolution bodies in Norway and is a member of the European Copyright Society, Academia Europea, and the Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters.
