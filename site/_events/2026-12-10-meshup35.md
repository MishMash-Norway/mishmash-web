This week, [Hans J. Skaug](/people/hans-skaug/) and [Shayan Dadman](/people/shayan-dadman/) will present *Studying Automatic Music Transcription Tools with Sonitra*.

## Abstract

Automatic music transcription (AMT) converts musical audio into sheet music and is useful to musicians at all levels of proficiency. The technology is making rapid progress due to recent developments in AI, especially large language models. Both open and proprietary AMT systems exist, but an objective comparison of these is lacking. We present Sonitra, a framework for benchmarking AMT, which is currently being developed as part of the MishMash project: [AI Transcription of Music](/projects/ai-transcription-of-music/). In this study, we use Sonitra to run AMTs, including but not limited to Basic Pitch and TransKun, on a suite of audio-MIDI paired datasets such as MAESTRO, where MIDI acts as the ground truth. Sonitra applies a chain of degradations to the audio, such as detuning and reverb, before running AMT. This allows us to quantify the effect of the various degradation factors and compare the various AMTs.

## Bios

Hans J. Skaug is a professor of statistics at the Department of Mathematics, University of Bergen. His research applies statistics and machine learning to biology and music information retrieval. He has experience with open-source software projects and is also a hobby musician.

Shayan Dadman is a researcher at UiT The Arctic University of Norway, where he earned his PhD in artificial intelligence. His work combines AI, music and human-computer interaction. He has built generative systems that use reinforcement learning to adapt to musicians’ intentions, studied how professional producers work with AI tools in the studio, and developed open-source methods for testing how well AI systems understand recorded music. His research explores how music AI can support creative practice while being evaluated rigorously and keeping human agency at the centre.
